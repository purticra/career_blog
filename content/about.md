## Hi, I'm Hongyi

I'm an Electrical Engineering student at Georgia Tech with a passion for building things that move, sense, and interact with the world around them. Currently maintaining a 4.00 GPA while working on ultrasonic phased arrays in the lab and on hardware prototyping outside it.

## What I Do

My work sits where acoustics, electronics, and mechanical design meet. Most of my time right now goes to ultrasound: driving transducer arrays so that sound can be aimed, and building the electronics and computation that let a beam be steered in real time. The rest is the hardware habit I came in with, which is taking an idea to a physical prototype through PCBs, firmware, CAD, and whatever fabrication process can produce the part that week.

I gravitate toward problems that force rapid iteration and where a measurement, rather than an opinion, settles whether the design works.

## My Background

Before Georgia Tech I spent time on projects in China. At Shanghai Huatai Automation I designed end joint control circuits and reworked mechanical structures for an imitation learning gripper. At Ningbo University's Intelligent Wireless Technology Lab I designed and fabricated substrate-integrated waveguide filters, optimized them in Ansys HFSS, and validated them on a vector network analyzer.

That RF work is closer to what I do now than it looks. A microwave filter and an ultrasonic phased array are both exercises in making a wave go where you want it and nowhere else.

My most ambitious early project was a bionic frog robot that maneuvered across water surfaces using pneumatic flexible actuators. It taught me biomimetic design and earned a spot showcasing at ISEF 2024 in Los Angeles.

## Current Focus

Since May 2026, most of my time has gone to the Flavin Neuromachines Lab at Georgia Tech, where I work as an undergraduate researcher on ultrasonic phased arrays and parametric audio: modulated ultrasound that renders audible sound in a narrow, steerable beam. My part covers literature review, system architecture selection, and getting the prototype running end to end.

Alongside the lab, I volunteer as a peer instructor at the Hive Makerspace and contribute hardware design to RoboRambler. Earlier this year my team won first place at the GT IEEE Robotech Hackathon with our MoonLine rover, where I served as chief mechanical designer, and I designed the piezoelectric module for µCHIMERA at the Inventure Prize 2026. I wrapped up my avionics PCB work with the Yellow Jacket Space Program in August 2026.
